## BSP event-stream experiment

over 1,500 tics and passed bidirectional cross-artifact recovery. Its paired
stage cell improved BSP mean by 16.917 percent and total render mean by

windows improved from 76.593/70.245 to 57.272/56.504 ms. The retained public
moving/firing result improved from 17.683 to 19.026 FPS, so this corrected
artifact was promoted while the larger fused-wall work continues.

The next successor also computes the exact normal angle, distance,
hypotenuse, endpoint scales, and scale step inside that same typed traversal.
It avoids re-entering the generated Java geometry path in `StoreWallRange`
while leaving the original Mocha wall setup, clipping, rasterization, plane,
drawseg, and masked-sprite side effects intact.

Correctness passed over 1,500 tics with zero frame mismatches, identical

### Production-entry promotion attempt

The incremental-deployment policy was exercised against the accepted masked
loop rather than waiting for a 30 FPS aggregate candidate.

The original `9752e327…` artifact could not be promoted: it selects the
stage-telemetry main class and intentionally omits authority/checkpoint
exports required by the retained coordinator. The production warmup caught
this fail-closed (`authorityEngine.canonicalState is not a function`); no
match was admitted and the prior renderer was restored with two READY slots.

A normal-entry production build, `ba71145d…` (1,719,428 bytes), passed the
full 1,500-tic pixel/state/checkpoint/cross-restore differential. Its public

post-rollback public measurement of the known-good `51aadc21…` renderer

This establishes a new promotion rule: stage-telemetry speed is diagnostic
only. Every incremental candidate must expose the production coordinator
contract, run through retained-slot warmup, and beat the deployed artifact in
the public unique-moving-frame test. Exact pixels alone do not imply equal
speed because TeaVM whole-module entry point and generated layout materially
affect compilation on the managed OCI venue.

A follow-up removed the retained Java fallback entirely and placed the same
exact typed loop inside the existing renderer class, avoiding the added bridge
class as an explanation. Artifact `0b710bf7…` again passed the complete
1,500-tic differential, but produced only 12.466 public FPS. It too was
rejected and rolled back. This confirms that the regression is generated
production-shape/compiler behavior, not execution of the fallback or a
separate bridge-class dispatch. The deployed `51aadc21…` source and two READY
slots were re-verified after rollback.

A content-addressed post-link experiment then replaced only the uniquely
identified `R_DrawColumnBoom.Indexed.invoke` function inside the deployed
artifact. The patcher fences the input SHA, exact definition boundary, class
registration, and removed exception path. It reduced the module by 1,081
bytes and passed the same 1,500-tic differential. Unlike either global
rebuild, it preserved the favorable production compilation tier, but its
public measurements were 17.565 FPS (including one 5.7-second lifecycle
stall) and 19.103 FPS on a warm repeat. That does not beat the 20.459 FPS
fresh deployed baseline, so the candidate was rejected and rolled back.

Post-link replacement is retained as a valid shape-preserving experiment
mechanism, but the masked-column loop alone lacks enough leverage. The next
use must target a larger ranked hot function and must still pass the public
promotion gate.

## Full typed BSP/wall-state pass

`0049-teavm-typed-bsp-wall-state-pass.patch` implemented the remaining
structural exact-render hypothesis. One typed MLE call owns subsector
`FindPlane`, wall `CheckPlane`, texture rasterization, inter-wall clip
mutation, opening allocation, and drawseg snapshots; Java receives only the
final visplane and drawseg state required by the unchanged plane/masked
stages.

The implementation exposed and closed four concrete parity defects before
measurement:

- the compact tape's distance/scale/scale-step fields were initially read
  from adjacent slots;
- an untextured wall has no light-table binding;
- valid masked-texture opening pointers may be negative and therefore cannot
  use sign as a null sentinel;
- `CheckPlane`'s returned split-plane ID must become the current subsector
  plane for the next wall.

A diagnostic stage comparison then matched the old renderer at every
boundary: the BSP framebuffer, 117 drawsegs, 889 opening values, 52
visplanes, the post-plane framebuffer, and the final framebuffer. The
instrumentation-free artifact
`7dda0bdf818fe0feb8b6f1184065b6e9b016ae3068e08e4610e39d1f1f8f0213`
passed both 300- and 1,500-tic differentials with zero frame/state mismatch,
raw checkpoint identity, and own/cross-artifact restore identity.

OCI performance rejected it:

## Current decision

The public OCI deployment remains the proven exact high-fidelity renderer
(`51aadc21bcb619928cee3e73217c8150cebf19aa3186dfd273ea3535d88e2edb`),
at approximately 19.6 FPS in its measured dense moving/firing windows. The
exact in-place masked-column change is retained as a building block and
raises a candidate to approximately 20.5–20.7 FPS, but is not independently
large enough to justify a production promotion.

The final structural exact-wall hypothesis has now also been implemented,
made exact, and rejected by dense OCI evidence. The current exact high-detail
ceiling on Always Free is therefore approximately 20.5–20.7 FPS with the
accepted in-place masked-column building block; no measured exact
single-session renderer shape reaches 30 FPS.

Further work must change a larger execution property rather than rearrange
the same raster loops: improve compiler digestibility without enlarging the
generated presentation artifact, divide work across an independently
available execution resource, or adopt a database-generated live frame
policy that preserves Doom's visible behavior without demanding a complete
fresh high-detail raster on every presented frame. The rejected compact
custom rasterizer remains out of consideration because it did not preserve
Doom's visuals.

## Held-forward stall reproduction and transport fix

The reported 10-30 second symptom was subsequently reproduced with the public
client under a 200 ms RTT / 10 Mbps downstream profile. Oracle accepted the
held-forward revision and continued producing changing full framebuffers, but
the client rendered only 24.783 FPS and recorded a 7,036.9 ms maximum canvas
gap. Eighty-three of 232 pixel requests were canceled.

Two client transport defects were responsible:

1. the 120 ms tail hedge treated ordinary WAN latency as a stranded request,
   duplicating reads against Always Free's single guaranteed API lane and
   canceling the losing copy;
2. after the finite 64-frame ring overran, the client waited for a full
   startup reserve before painting the new confirmed suffix. A
   bandwidth-limited client could overrun again before building that reserve,
   turning one recoverable gap into repeated resynchronization and a frozen
   canvas.

The deployed client now launches a hedge only after 750 ms and paints the
first available confirmed database frame immediately after a genuine ring
overrun, rebuilding reserve through the occupancy controller. It still never
predicts, invents, or reorders a frame. Under the same stressed profile,
ordinary duplicate cancellations fell from 83 to zero in the isolated hedge
cell, and the resynchronization change reduced the worst observed canvas gap
from 7,036.9 ms to 855.5 ms. The 10 Mbps profile remains bandwidth-limited
and is not a 30 FPS qualification result.

## Two-view temporal diagnostics

The multiplayer target was explicitly separated from solo: solo retains its
30 FPS database-frame gate, while two simultaneous independent viewpoints may
qualify at 20 FPS. The acceptance harness now accepts only the explicit values
20 or 30 and derives its cadence tails from that selected budget; 30 remains
the default.

Neither measured candidate qualified:

| Candidate | Exact keyframe interval | Player 0 | Player 1 | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `0c2e1c7d...` | 2 | 16.476 FPS | 16.516 FPS | reject |
| `a65fee9e...` | 3 | 18.786 FPS | 18.796 FPS | reject |

The interval-3 p95 gaps were 148.7 and 161.5 ms. All 300 captured
framebuffers for each player were unique, consecutive, database-authored
frames, and common-tic POV digests remained different. The failure is
therefore producer burst/starvation, not a repeated-frame or collapsed-POV
defect. The candidates were not promoted; production was restored to the
pinned `d3d0571a...` coordinator after the diagnostic.

One retained coverage item is explicit: when a palette or layout changes, the
temporal fallback intentionally copies the newer exact pixels for the
intermediate tics. No such transition occurred in these 300-frame cells, so a
damage/pickup-flash route must separately verify whether adjacent visual
duplicates are acceptable or need palette-aware synthesis.

An independent xhigh audit confirmed the burst shape from raw samples.
Interval 2 alternated roughly 17 ms and 104–107 ms gaps; interval 3 delivered
roughly 17, 31, then 96–112 ms. The browser's selected reserve never became a
real reserve: it repeatedly drained to zero, while 622 pixel exchanges served
about 200 useful interval-3 bundles.

Three narrower remedies were measured and rejected:

- a multiplayer-only 49 ms clock, three-frame reserve, starvation deadline
  reset, and bundle-aware poll delay reduced empty polling but left production
  at 18.73 FPS with a 101.0 ms p95 gap;
- persisting one DPB2 bundle per player reduced temporal locator calls from
  three to two, but regressed to 17.47 FPS because viewpoint extraction and
  response tails grew;
- interval-4 exact keyframes plus four-tic-aware polling reached only
  18.38 FPS and a 95.1 ms p95 gap.

The near-flat interval-3/interval-4 result shows that separate 128,016-byte
DPD1 persistence for every synthesized tic is now a first-class ceiling.
Lowering exact-render frequency again is not justified. The remaining
high-fidelity persistence experiment is one combined multi-tic/two-POV
locator, retaining the old exact renderer at its keyframes and extracting the
authenticated player view only on the read side.
